# Expert-group zero masks at the down-MMQ observation boundary

The [single-slot static-permutation result](qwen-moe-static-zero-permutation.md) leaves a practical question: do its zero blocks survive when a *single expert image* is consumed for multiple prompt rows? On the actual layer-0 routed post-SwiGLU captures, **the original-float zero mask is identical for every selected row of each expert**. All 169 train experts and 188 held experts have zero within-expert mask variation; all 153 experts seen in both splits have exactly the same 512-bit mask across splits. The values themselves differ. This is an observed finite-domain invariant, not a proof for every possible model input or layer. The 35 held-only experts (76 slots) have no train mask. In a spot-check of 14 selected official-BF16 experts among IDs 0–15, neither the gate nor up tensor has an entirely zero 2,048-weight row; the capture's zeros are not explained by zero rows in those original gate/up weights. Their mechanism should be checked before calling this a global architectural property.

The observation makes the group-level combinatorics unusually simple. For an expert tile containing rows \(h_1,\ldots,h_m\), a skippable 32-coordinate block under a *new* column layout must be contained in \(Z=\bigcap_i\{c:h_i[c]=0\}\). Thus at most \(\lfloor |Z|/32\rfloor\) disjoint blocks can be skipped, and a free tile-specific permutation that puts \(Z\) first attains that bound. When the rows share one mask, this equals each row's own oracle bound, irrespective of the MMQ row width. A static expert permutation trained on any one of the observed train rows also attains it on every observed row of that expert. This is a checked finite-capture construction and bound, not a bit-identity proof for changing Q5_K weight groups or Q8_1 activation scales.

| Held / 128 | 188 | 0 | 871 | 1,136 | 2.544% |

Counts are skipped *K blocks per expert tile*, out of sixteen possible per tile. J=64 is the selected native whole-prompt expert width, and the held layer-0 126-token capture has at most 29 rows assigned to one expert, so J=64 and J=128 have the same tiles. At J=64 the static layout reaches **871/1,136 = 76.7%** of the hindsight bound; *all* 265 missing blocks belong to the 35 experts unseen in train. For the 153 shared experts the static layout attains every oracle block on the held rows. The group oracle's 1,136/3,008 = 37.77% block share is larger than the per-slot oracle's 36.68% because its denominator weights experts once, rather than by their routed row frequency. It is not a larger amount of recoverable whole-model traffic.

The last column deliberately overpays the opportunity: it multiplies skipped *tile-block* fractions by the entire eight-expert down-image share, `40 * 5,767,168 / 2,626,187,904`, as though these groups represented all forty layers and removing one block removed the same fraction of each token's compulsory one-read image bytes. MMQ can already reuse an expert image across multiple rows; actual image reads, R/I tiling and DRAM traffic need not follow this fraction. The observer grants free mask tests, zero metadata, image permutation and arbitrary selective weight reads, and charges no activation-remap cost. Neither the installed dense image nor its MMQ kernel skips any such block, and rearranging individual columns changes packed scale groups and FP32 arithmetic. Even granting every missing expert a free hindsight mask, the conditional ceiling here is **3.317%** of one-read whole-model bytes at J=64, before any real cost. This explains why the stable masks are structurally interesting but do not yet justify a Q5_K native sparsity port over the larger ordinary Q8 and gate/up costs.

The better next question is whether the apparent expert-fixed mask is generated by an exact, input-independent property of the selected GGUF consumer or only holds on these short layer-0 text captures. Inspect gate/up outputs immediately before SwiGLU and broaden the captured route/producer panel to other layers and generated tokens. If it remains exact, *then* build a paid joint weight/activation layout and assess complete-model held language loss at a matched image rate before native timing. Do not extrapolate the measured zero pattern to an unchanged exact installed arithmetic map.
