# Actual routed expert span: a finite hindsight bound

Can the eight selected expert directions be reduced by reweighting the remaining **unchanged** outputs, rather than simply dropping the missing outputs? The [complete all-layer capture](qwen-moe-all-producers.md) contains both disjoint 64-token real-text prefixes' eight selected down vectors and normalized router scores at every one of forty Qwen3.6-35B-A3B layers. The selected GGUF callback cuts fusion, so this is an actual quantized-producer local-map question, not a native full-model speed or quality experiment.

For each layer/token, define `v_i = float64(score_i) * float64(down_i)` and `y = sum_i v_i`. For each nonempty subset `S` of the eight, give an oracle **the entire target `y`**, let it independently rescale every retained `v_i` by an arbitrary real number, and minimize `||y - sum_{i in S} c_i v_i||₂`. Enumerate all 254 proper nonempty subsets. If `G_ij = v_i · v_j`, the best residual is `||y||² - (G[S,:]1)ᵀ G[S,S]⁻¹ (G[S,:]1)`; the script checks selected projections directly in 2,048 dimensions. This is the exact Euclidean optimum for the stated finite captured vectors and unrestricted scalar coefficients in real arithmetic (evaluated FP64). A subset can be extended to seven vectors without increasing its best error, so the minimum seven-vector error certifies the **entire family** of one-or-more unchanged-direction suppressions at a given local threshold. This does not prove a native FP32 identity: optimal real coefficients change the arithmetic contract, and even the observed sum is computed offline in FP64.

| Train | .09947 | .29523 | **0** | 138 | .15837% |
| Held | **.09778** | **.28231** | **0** | **158** | **.18007%** |

The held best-seven **median individual** local relative error is .11985; the highest-scored seven with freely fit coefficients have .11547 aggregate RMS, versus .09778 for best-subset hindsight. Four vectors with arbitrary hindsight coefficients leave .28231 aggregate RMS even with a free best-subset choice; the highest-scored four leave .30910. Only **5/2,560** held layer/token decisions can retain four under 5% local relative error. At 5% the adaptive held oracle omits nothing in 2,441 decisions, one in 98, two in 10, three in 6, four in 4 and six in 1. At 10% it saves 1.33440% conditional whole-model one-read bytes. The [unchanged-coefficient omission oracle](qwen-moe-all-layer-subsets.md) saved 149 held assignments/.16940% at 5%; granting arbitrary reweighting and optimal subset choice changes that to just 158/.18007%. Both oracles save **zero** at 1%.

For a retained set `S`, independent real scalar fit cannot do better than its orthogonal projection on the span of the unchanged vectors. All smaller retained subsets are contained in a seven-vector subset, establishing the zero-at-1% bound for this entire stated family on these **5,120 observed layer/token cases**. This bound does **not** cover changing expert weight directions, learning a new shared representation, a vector-valued correction from uncomputed producers, a downstream observation weaker than the routed sum, or other prompts. A 5% layer-local Euclidean error is not an acceptable whole-model loss budget. The optimistic byte fraction divides skipped expert images (1,900,544–2,039,808 bytes depending on layer) by 2,626,187,904 conditional one-read bytes/token and charges *nothing* for computing oracle coefficients, choosing subsets, router/dispatch, or physical rereads. An online implementation would also have to infer those coefficients without computing omitted outputs; no such implementation is proposed.

The result changes the next question: do **not** port a scalar-reweighting selector for frozen expert outputs on these captured prefixes. Instead change learned expert directions/codes or their shared producer/consumer coordinates, build a paid complete forty-layer image using broader actual routes, then measure disjoint complete-model language loss and native time. The independent ordinary Q8/expert unprofiled phase question remains an engine target.
