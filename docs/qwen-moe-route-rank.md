# A route-specific output basis is still broad

Kelana's [route-conditioned rank study](../../kelana/research/moe/route-rank/README.md) asks whether knowing the eight experts before their down projections repairs the poor common-output rank seen on the official Qwen3.6-35B-A3B BF16 tensors. It gives the observer a separate optimal output basis for each route at no selection cost. Under independent isotropic expert hidden vectors and equal fixed scores, the best rank-512 projection still loses .606, .582 and .599 relative RMS on three eight-of-sixteen layer-0 routes. The universal sixteen-expert basis loses .673, .675 and .675 on the same routes. At rank 1024, even the route oracle loses .342, .310 and .332 relative RMS and uses 75% of direct routed-down logical MACs before basis selection and scattering.

The proof is an exact optimum *within orthogonal rank-r output projections* for the stated hidden distribution. It does not constrain actual producer-reachable hiddens, trained codes, the next layer's observation, or a nonlinear packed consumer. A catalogue is costly even on these sixteen experts: 12,870 eight-expert routes at 2 MiB per BF16 rank-512 basis means 26.99 GB of bases before route-specific expert factors, versus 33.55 MB for the sixteen original BF16 down tensors. Dynamic bases instead require online construction, unpriced here.

The source-hashed CPU receipts and reproduction command live in `../../data/qwen-moe/route-rank/` and the Kelana report. This is a measured negative for frozen route-keyed common output projection on the synthetic isotropic domain, not a native or whole-model speed measurement. Bonsai's Qwen runtime and service did not change. The next useful question is whether real producer hiddens and scores on disjoint text sharply concentrate the *composed* sum. Only then is a paid quantized basis/factor image and native packed consumer warranted. The dense sub-bit model's complete-image NLL failures warn against shipping on down-output RMS alone.
