# Global Q8 scale/code split loses in a compiled gfx1151 wave reader

The [zero-padding global split-bank construction](qwen-moe-q8-global-soa.md) reaches the separate 32/64/128-byte instruction-sector floors, but reducing those modeled requests is not sufficient to improve execution. A same-byte, same-dot standalone HIP reader on gfx1151 is slower than the installed interleaved Q8_0 coordinate on three image sizes. This is a **model-less native consumer negative**, not a whole-model speed claim or a reason to rule out a different reader schedule.

`bench/qwen_q8_layout.hip` constructs 272 bytes per eight Q8_0 blocks in both layouts: the installed `[FP16 scale, 32 int8 codes] × 8` wave group and separate contiguous code/scale banks. Every wave owns a K=8192 output row, loops over 32 groups and makes the same 256 ordered lane-wise FP32 FMA updates per group, then the same warp fold. Codes and FP16 scales are generated identically and checked through the **complete output**: zero differing FP32 words at all three sizes. Synthetic activation and weights are deliberately not Qwen model inputs. Both consumer bodies compile to 32 VGPR, no flat scratch; the split uses 18 SGPR versus 15 interleaved in the saved gfx1151 assembly. This is a direct compiled cost comparison for this wave grammar, not the selected llama.cpp MMVQ body's instruction census or its exact FP32 contract.

| Q8 bytes per arm | Output rows | Interleaved median | Global split median | Split cost |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8,912,896 | 1,024 | 0.056119 ms | 0.060158 ms | +7.20% |
| 71,303,168 | 8,192 | 0.715534 ms | 0.893889 ms | +24.93% |
| 1,492,910,080 (selected Q8 bank size) | 171,520 | 15.030141 ms | 15.362609 ms | +2.21% |

Each row reports five alternating interleaved/split device-event samples in one bounded process. Full-size samples are 15.025481–15.060567 versus 15.309211–15.381568 ms, all split slower. The 71 MB arm is 0.703969–0.737654 versus 0.884889–0.917440 ms. The quiet 8.9/71 MB panels ran at ~636–637 MHz median GPU clock; the full arm at ~687 MHz with 1.2 extra host cores flagged by the wrapper, but its paired samples remain directionally separated. The attempted fixed-clock full panel was host-contaminated (15.3 busy cores, power limited) and is **excluded** from the receipt and table rather than folded into a favorable or unfavorable median. These clocks are far below selected full-model execution and do not price a Qwen inference step.

**Decision:** do not directly port this global split reader solely because its abstract sector count reaches a floor. It requires a second address stream and more scalar registers, while separate instruction sectors do not imply DRAM-byte reductions; these panels measure the combined actual consequence under one specific wave schedule. A better independent next experiment is to splice a wave-local or global code/scale reader into the selected MMVQ *with its real activation preparation and graph*, then require bitwise full-head/state identity and matched single-stream decode, prompt and 32-stream timings before adoption. If the native reader is slower, investigate compiled address work and real cache behavior rather than pursuing a still lower abstract sector count. Keep the image's same bytes and arithmetic map; no model image, executable selection, service default or numerical contract changed here.

[Source, executable, assembly, wrapper logs and per-round receipt](../../data/qwen-moe/q8-layout-native/receipt.json) are in the existing data directory.
