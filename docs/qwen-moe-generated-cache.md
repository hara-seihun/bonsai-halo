# Finite global expert-image cache on generated Qwen routes

A 32 MiB *global* image cache cannot realize the 8.96–9.26% one-read-byte opportunity of free, simultaneous retention of eight previous images **at each of forty layers**. This is a capacity result for complete packed expert images, not a DRAM measurement or a bound on consumer fusion or changed representations. The subsequent [arbitrary-granularity capacity proof](qwen-moe-cache-capacity.md) extends the unchanged one-read serial-token family to cache lines and subimages, tightening its cold bound to 1.25107%.

## Contract and proof

The selected Qwen GGUF has 256 disjoint expert images per layer and eight distinct expert IDs per layer and token. For every chronological generated-token step, layers run in order. The complete gate/up/down packed image for an expert costs 1,900,544 bytes in 37 layers, 2,039,808 in three layers (inventory `traffic.json`). Assume a cold 32 MiB fully associative cache, free bypass, free replacement and advance knowledge of the whole route; let unrelated nonexpert and activation traffic bypass it. Charge a cache hit only when the **entire** selected image is resident; an image is requested once per token, because the eight IDs in each layer are distinct. Grant free reordering of the eight experts within a layer.

At a token boundary, or at any instant during a token, the cache holds at most 17 complete expert images: `18 × 1,900,544 = 34,209,792 > 33,554,432`. An image accessed in the current token cannot be accessed again in that token. Thus every complete-image hit in a token must correspond to an image that was resident at the start of the token; at most 17 hits per token after the cold first token. This bound does not require independence or stationarity of the routes and holds with arbitrary clairvoyant scheduling. At most `17 × 2,039,808 = 34,676,736` bytes of modeled expert image reads can be removed per later token. Against the declared complete one-read stream of 2,626,187,904 bytes/token, a 48-step cold panel saves at most `47 × 34,676,736 / (48 × 2,626,187,904) = 1.292912%`. The steady-state bound is 1.3204%. Free eight-image-per-layer retention instead needs 611,516,416 bytes, over 18 times this cache's nominal capacity. The byte ceiling deliberately gives *all* 17 hits the larger image, even though only three layers have it.

This bound assumes an indivisible full image, a one-read-per-expert baseline, a resident GPU cache of the stated size and one serial token at a time. The [separate byte-granularity bound](qwen-moe-cache-capacity.md) covers cache lines/subimages within that unchanged one-read serial family. Changes to the expert image or arithmetic schedule, cross-token fusion/speculation, and different baselines remain outside both bounds. It is not an ISA lower bound, nor a proof that a cache can deliver the upper bound at zero synchronization cost. The selected runtime already groups prompt experts, while serial generation is causal. No code path, model image or numerical map changed.

## Actual generated-route check

`tools/qwen-moe/generated_cache_bound.py` replays both 48-step, forty-layer top-k captures from [generated routes](qwen-moe-generated-routes.md), with source/input/model hashes in [`global-cache.json`](../../data/qwen-moe/generated-routes/global-cache.json). An offline next-use policy allowing image bypass gets 752 complete-image hits at 16 uniform slots and 799 at 17 slots in **each** split (respectively 16 or 17 hits on each of the 47 later tokens). Sixteen *largest* images fit within 32 MiB, so the 16-slot replay is a feasible cache-capacity schedule given free oracle future IDs and free control. Seventeen *smallest* images fit but seventeen largest do not, so the 17-slot replay upper-bounds hit **count** for every capacity-respecting full-image cache; pricing each at the maximum image size gives the rigorous byte bound above. At sixteen slots the actual image sizes of hits sum to 1,430,044,672 train / 1,429,209,088 held bytes out of the complete 126,057,019,392-byte 48-step stream (~1.134%). A conventional sixteen-image LRU has zero hits: the other 39 layers displace the whole previous layer's images before its next visit. This zero is a property of this full-image sequential-access model, not observed GPU misses.

For fixed equal-sized slots and free bypass, keeping the nearest next-use images after each access is optimal for hit count by the farthest-future exchange: replacing a retained later-use image by an earlier-use one cannot reduce hits up through the earlier access, then their roles may be swapped. The 16/17-slot simulations use that policy. Image sizes are not equal, so the 17-slot number is an optimistic cardinality ceiling rather than the exact byte-optimal 32 MiB cache. Since both captures saturate the 17-hit per-token bound, no finer route predictor raises this family ceiling. Capturing the fused no-cut route remains necessary before claiming the IDs represent the selected numerical map, but does not weaken the capacity proof.

**Next useful question:** determine actual per-expert Q4/Q5 DRAM transactions and within-token cache-line reuse in unprofiled native serial decode. Mere cross-token subimage retention within the same one-read image cannot recover the free forty-layer reuse scenario: [even arbitrary byte-sized cached fragments save at most 1.25107% of complete modeled bytes cold](qwen-moe-cache-capacity.md). A changed packed layout, direct consumer or independent-stream batching is a different family. Whole-model prompt and multi-stream speed still require independent acceptance.
