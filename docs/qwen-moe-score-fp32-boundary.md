# The seven-score real coordinate needs a rounding correction at the native FP32 boundary

[Kelana's exact rank-seven witness](../../kelana/research/moe/score-observation-rank/README.md) concerns an ideal seven-dimensional normalized-score simplex. The eight *captured* selected-runtime binary32 scores are individually rounded and need not sum to exactly one. I tested the concrete shortcut `a0 = float32(1 - left_fold_float32(a1,...,a7))` over **all 5,120 actual layer/token observations** from two disjoint 64-token forty-layer producer captures. This is an offline test of a score interface, not another router exponential approximation.

| Split | Rows whose left-folded eight-score sum is not exactly 1 | Reconstructed score 0 differs | Weighted sums changed in exact-real arithmetic | Relative RMS sum error | Maximum score-0 error | Score-0 correction in FP32 ULPs |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Train | 1,021 / 2,560 | 2,205 / 2,560 | 2,205 / 2,560 | 1.35947e-7 | 1.78814e-7 | -12..11 |
| Held | 1,020 / 2,560 | 2,159 / 2,560 | 2,159 / 2,560 | 1.49306e-7 | 1.78814e-7 | -11..11 |

Every changed row changes the *complete* 2,048-coordinate exact-real weighted sum, not merely the omitted score: all captured first down-output vectors have nonzero coordinates and the change is precisely `(original_a0 - reconstructed_a0)*down_0` with the other seven terms fixed. The captured train/held coordinate-change counts are 4,515,840 / 4,421,632. This is an exact arithmetic statement about saved binary32 operands, not an assertion that the installed GPU multiplies and accumulates in the host's FP64 order. Relative RMS reports the size of the changed-map effect; it is **not** held language loss or permission to ship that map.

There is also a checked finite-domain *lossless* construction. All captured scores are positive, so their unsigned binary32 bit patterns are ordered by value. Reconstruct the omitted score by the declared seven-score FP32 fold, then add a signed correction to its **integer bit pattern**. Across all 5,120 observations the correction lies in `[-12,11]`; one signed byte (indeed five fixed bits on this finite capture) reconstructs every omitted FP32 word bit-for-bit. Seven FP32 words plus a byte would occupy 29 rather than 32 bytes per score row **if** such rows were independently stored, at the price of seven FP32 additions, a subtraction, bit conversion/correction, and an additional byte read. At forty layers the *most* saved score-storage traffic is 120 bytes/token, or **0.00000457%** of the 2,626,187,904-byte conditional whole-model one-read comparator, before any score consumer overhead. The selected runtime does not have a separately established 32-byte score read per layer to eliminate; this is a conditional storage calculation, not a native bandwidth or speedup measurement. Neither the signed-byte correction bound nor its reconstruction scheme is universal outside these captured inputs; a general lossless implementation must explicitly handle correction overflow (or retain the eighth word).

The rank-seven smooth-real theorem and this finite-word result answer different questions. Rank seven is dimension-minimal for the ideal simplex observation with fixed outputs. Dropping the eighth rounded native word and inferring it from a unit sum **does not preserve the actual finite-word score interface**. A joint score/producer packed coordinate remains possible, but an exact native consumer must also retain the missing rounding distinction and the installed FP32 operation order. A new score-buffer compressor is not an engine priority for 120 conditional bytes/token; ordinary unprofiled Q8/expert physical time and a changed paid expert image on broad producers are more consequential independent questions.
