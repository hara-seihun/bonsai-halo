# Paired expert timing in the installed Qwen decode trace

The counter-free, depth-1024 trace has a sharp two-mode pattern within identical routed kernel shapes. Across eight measured tokens and forty layers, 256 Q4 gate/up calls last 47.838–67.477 µs; the other 64 last 89.757–123.276 µs. The Q4 launch has the same `mul_mat_vec_q<Q4_K,1,true,true,...>` instantiation and 16,384 × 8 grid throughout. Pairing the corresponding routed down call, router top-k and fused shared-expert Q8 call by layer ordinal shows that the long calls are not isolated to the Q4 consumer.

| Layer calls | Count | Router top-k µs | Routed gate/up µs | Routed down µs | Shared Q8 µs |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Q4 ≤80 µs | 256 | 4.752 | 50.427 | 38.667 | 13.963 |
| Q4 >80 µs | 64 | 18.084 | 113.803 | 87.507 | 19.417 |

The entries are means of HIP device-event durations, not host launch gaps. In the slow-Q4 group, 57/64 routers also exceed 10 µs and 58/64 down calls exceed 65 µs. None of the 256 ordinary-Q4 calls has a down call over 65 µs. Even top-k, which does not read the expert weight bank, moves from a 4.399 µs ordinary median to 19.72 µs in the slow-Q4 group. Seven ordinary-Q4 layers have a slow router: the slow period can begin or end between adjacent dispatches. Several layer indices recur across later tokens, notably 1, 3, 6, 13, 19 and 26; the first token has only one slow Q4 call. This is a correlated execution regime, not a uniform extra instruction cost attributable to the expert kernels. Its cause is open. Cache effects, clocks, concurrent activity and GPU scheduling need a controlled native experiment to distinguish them.

Replacing each slow pair's measured duration with the ordinary-group mean subtracts **0.898 ms/token** from the trace's 4.462 ms/token routed-expert kernel total. That descriptive difference is 46.4% of the earlier 1.935 ms/token gap to the conditional one-read, 242 GB/s comparator. It is **not** an attainable speedup or a measured traffic saving. Even in the ordinary group, forty Q4/down pairs would cost about 3.564 ms/token; the same conditional comparator still leaves about 1.037 ms/token before other work. A consumer port justified by treating all 1.935 ms as fixed Q4/Q5 instruction overhead has the wrong premise. A paired full-model experiment must report the frequency of the slow regime separately from within-regime kernel changes; otherwise a change in episode frequency can masquerade as a faster dot product.

The installed source already groups assignments by expert in `mul_mat_id`. The trace includes all eight assignments, their weighted sum, the original nonexpert arithmetic and the full output head. Its Q4 calls are one per layer, not eight independent per-expert launches. The analysis excludes two synthetic-depth setup head evaluations, checks eight 1,565-dispatch decode spans and forty Q4, down, router and fused shared-expert Q8 calls per span. The Q5/Q6 down quantization mix remains unchanged. [The analyzer](../tools/qwen-moe/expert_latency.py) reproduces the per-layer modes and source/input hashes from the [retained trace and installed receipt](../../data/qwen-moe/q8-unprofiled/receipt.json); its [JSON result](../../data/qwen-moe/q8-unprofiled/expert-latency.json) keeps every layer's slow count. No GPU was used in this secondary analysis, and no runtime, model image, service or numerical map changed.
