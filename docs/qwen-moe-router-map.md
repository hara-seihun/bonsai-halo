# The routed-score observer needs eight exponentials over the reals, but not under the native FP32 contract

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's forty routers select eight of 256 experts and normalize the selected scores. The selected GGUF graph builds a 256-way softmax, top-k, gather, eight-score sum/clamp/div. Its single-token CUDA fusion computes a 256-way warp-local softmax and a separate eight-score normalization. Both routes expend exponentials on 248 scores whose values the selected weighted sum does not consume.

For finite real logits `z`, no selection bias and positive denominator, let `S` be the eight largest coordinates. Softmax preserves the coordinate order, and

`(exp(z_i-m)/sum_j exp(z_j-m)) / sum_{k in S}(exp(z_k-m)/sum_j exp(z_j-m)) = exp(z_i-mS)/sum_{k in S} exp(z_k-mS)` for `i in S`.

Thus the **complete routed-score observation** (ordered IDs and normalized selected scores) factors through the ordered eight logits. No 256-way exponential is required in a *real-arithmetic* lowering. The Qwen graph has no expert-selection bias, uses softmax gating and requests selected-score normalization (`src/models/qwen35moe.cpp:502`; `src/llama-graph.cpp:2076–2195` in the pinned native source). The identity does not assert FP32 bit equality, and a selected-only kernel must still read and rank all 256 logits. It removes 248 exponentials per token per layer under that grammar, not 248 logit reads, expert products or model operations. The reference's clamp never fires on these observed nonzero scores; a general map would need to retain its specified zero/underflow behavior.

## Real producer comparison

`capture_router_logits.cpp` captures only `ffn_moe_logits-0` on each of the frozen 113-train/126-held real-text prompt rows from the installed Qwen on-demand runtime. The callback graph view ends **before** the softmax; an earlier no-read panel showed that this boundary does not remove the top-k fusion. The separately retained original layer-0 capture contains selected IDs, normalized scores and all eight down outputs on the identical text/token fixtures; that capture requested score tensors and thereby **cut the router fusion**, so its FP32 weights are the unfused map, not selected fused scores. Both were captured before and after the J=64 prompt-width source revision respectively; the installed acceptance establishes bit-identical logits for that revision on its acceptance panel, and this comparison checks the selected IDs against the newly captured logits on every row. It does not claim paired bit identity of the two capture processes' intermediate pre-router logits.

The CPU construction selects the eight largest FP32 logits and applies one FP32 eight-way max/exp/sum/div. On **all 239 rows**, its selected IDs match the captured eight IDs in order. Relative to the unfused captured scores, it changes **561/904 train and 585/1008 held score bits**. Maximum absolute score error is 5.96e-8 in each split. With the captured eight down vectors and an FP64 weighted-sum observer, the score substitution changes the train and held local routed sum by **7.17e-8 and 6.45e-8 relative RMS**; maximum per-token relative norm is 1.36e-7 and 1.54e-7. A NumPy FP32 emulation of the dense two-normalization formula also differs on 485/904 and 539/1008 scores: its exp/reduction order is **not** the GPU reference either. The changed bits demonstrate why algebraic cancellation cannot certify the installed arithmetic, while the local perturbation bounds the *observed* score-only approximation on these producers. It is not complete-model quality or a measured speedup.
