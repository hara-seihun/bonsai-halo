# Qwen's eight-way sum is already one native kernel

I priced a proposed fused score-weighted expert sum before writing a kernel. The selected Qwen3.6-35B-A3B runtime already fuses the seven `ggml_add` nodes of each routed sum into **one** `k_bin_bcast` launch per layer. The graph applies selected weights either before or after the expert body, then adds the eight views in selection order. CUDA's `ggml_cuda_op_fused_add` recognizes the chain, and its variadic fold evaluates those seven additions in that order. This is not eight separately launched adds waiting to be replaced.

The [installed depth-1024 trace](../../data/qwen-moe/q8-unprofiled/receipt.json) has exactly forty seven-add kernels per decode token, one per layer, alongside eighty binary multiplies. On seven warm tokens, the forty fused reductions total **0.125379 ms/token** out of **20.846359 ms/token** summed device duration, or 0.6014%. The eighty multiplies total 0.205336 ms/token. Some layers apply their routing weight before the down projection, so it would be wrong to attribute forty of those multiplies to a uniform post-down stage. Even granting a zero-cost replacement for *all eighty* binary multiplies plus the already-fused routed sum removes at most **0.330715 ms/token**, 1.59% of this traced device work. This is an intentionally generous conditional bound, not a wall-time bound or attainable speedup. It cannot explain the 4.462 ms/token routed expert matvec phase.

The source identity is installed revision `b4c67ced9f6bac6b1661b25714946d999374e06f`. In its `src/llama-graph.cpp`, the graph builds a left-associated loop of seven `ggml_add` nodes, with a selected `ggml_mul` after down only when `weight_before_ffn` is false. `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/ggml-cuda.cu` recognizes the add chain and calls `ggml_cuda_op_fused_add`; `binbcast.cu` folds the extra operands into one kernel. The trace's 40 `k_bin_bcast<op_add,..., seven operand pointers>` launches per token match that path. A second 40 add launches with two operand pointers belongs to other graph work and is not counted as the routed reduction. Source and trace identities, every measured token's device durations and the producer capture hashes are in [the analysis receipt](../../data/qwen-moe/sum-fusion/receipt.json), produced by [`sum_fusion_bound.py`](../tools/qwen-moe/sum_fusion_bound.py). The profiler's host gaps are excluded.

Reordering the sum is a distinct numerical map. On 113 train and 126 held real layer-0 producer tokens, I multiplied each captured FP32 down vector by its captured normalized FP32 router score, then compared a left fold with a balanced tree using host FP32 operations. The tree changes 121,425 of 231,424 train components and 135,421 of 258,048 held components; all 239 tokens have at least one different component. Held relative RMS difference is `6.94e-8`, and maximum absolute difference is `7.45e-9`. This is a witness against claiming FP32 bit equality merely from real-number associativity, not a native HIP bit comparison or evidence of harmful language quality. The native decoder's down output and score capture do not themselves certify the host's multiply instruction or compiler contraction.

A new standalone reduction kernel is the wrong next port. If a direct packed expert consumer can avoid writing each down vector, fuse score multiplication and the same ordered sum into that consumer and measure its *whole expert path*, gathering and scatter included. Otherwise the available prize is in Q8/Q4/Q5 consumption and its shared slow execution regime, not in the already-fused seven adds. No runtime, model image, GPU reservation or resident service changed in this CPU analysis.
