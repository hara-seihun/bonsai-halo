# Causal expert persistence on serial Qwen generation

The [batched-prompt route result](qwen-moe-route-prefetch.md) predicted 38.53% next-row expert overlap across all forty layers, but it could not tell whether that pattern survives autoregressive generation. This panel continues **48 actual greedy single-token steps** from each of two disjoint real-text prefixes (64 tokens each), on the pinned Qwen3.6-35B-A3B UD-Q4_K_M image and the selected on-demand library `3f552c2`. It captures eight selected IDs at every layer and step. Each process first runs the same continuation with capture disabled, clears its context state, and then runs with capture enabled; all 48 greedy IDs agree within each pair. The observer requests the top-k view and thus **cuts graph fusion** in the observed arm. Matching generated IDs does not prove identical hidden states or routes in the uninstrumented target. This measures a serial generated route under the callback map, not the exact selected serving map.

For consecutive generated rows, retain the preceding row's eight full expert images *at the same layer*, and count its intersection with the next row. The train-text continuation has **5,394/15,040 assignments (35.86%)** in that set; the disjoint held-text continuation has **5,982/15,040 (39.77%)**. The train two temporal halves contribute 2,720/2,674 overlaps, held 3,115/2,867. Held layer 0 contributes just 20/376 assignments over 47 transitions, while layer 31 contributes 202/376; the depth effect in the prompt panel survives in these two serial continuations. This is a causal reuse count, not an oracle that chooses the current route.

At the installed per-layer full packed-image sizes, free lossless retention would avoid **11,426,291,712 logical routed-image bytes** on the 47 held transitions (10,302,365,696 on train). Dividing by 47 times the declared 2,626,187,904-byte complete one-read token budget gives a conditional **9.257% held (8.347% train)** whole-model weight-byte ceiling. It is slightly larger than the held prompt extrapolation's 8.96%, rather than collapsing to layer 0's near-zero reuse. This is a conditional byte ceiling only: the 47 transitions request 15,040 images, images may be fetched in fragments or served by another cache, and native grouping/physical DRAM transactions were not observed. No throughput or accepted arithmetic-map change follows.

**Why a pointer cache is not the construction.** One token needs eight full images from each of forty distinct expert banks. Retaining all forty previous sets simultaneously means 320 images and approximately 611.5 MB of packed weights, versus 32 MB of last-level cache. The next visit to a given layer follows the other layers and nonexpert consumers. Under the declared full-image and one-read execution grammar, those intervening distinct images exceed cache capacity many times; merely remembering their addresses cannot realize the 9.257% ceiling. A concrete next experiment should instrument the fused top-k **without changing the graph**, sample actual DRAM transactions for routed expert weights across successive decode steps, and test a layout or schedule that can keep useful tiles live across the intervening work. If those measured transactions already exploit retention, do not count these logical hits again as a new speedup. Another short callback capture or trained fixed-route predictor does not resolve this question.
