# Actual Q5_K expert down gains

[Kelana's layer-0 study](../../kelana/research/moe/routed-down-gains/README.md) decodes the first sixteen down experts from the pinned UD-Q4_K_M GGUF rather than replacing them with a hypothetical four-bit image. On separate 96-input synthetic train and held panels, the Q5_K routed sum has .030326/.029931 relative RMS against official BF16 down responses using official BF16 gate/up hidden vectors. A joint 16-scalar gain fitted to the *whole weighted sum* lowers train error to .030324 but raises held error to .029934. The paid FP16-gain arm raises held error to .029935; independent FP16 gains also lose. The gain family adds 512 bytes for all 256 experts per layer and eight router-coefficient multiplies per token, before native routing overhead. The unrestricted train-optimal fit has no useful held margin here.

This is a CPU result on synthetic shared Gaussian inputs, uniformly selected eight-of-sixteen routes and independent softmax-normal scores. It is not a quality study on actual router outputs, does not touch the shared expert, and cannot be presented as a whole-model or native speed comparison. The [source and image-hashed receipt](../../data/qwen-moe/routed-down-gains/README.md) and the proof of the restricted optimum are linked from Kelana. No runtime, installed executable or service changed. The next experiment should capture real hidden vectors and routes, then compare equal-byte expert-aware down codes under the weighted sum and held model loss; frozen scalar corrections are not that experiment.
